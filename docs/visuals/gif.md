# GIF / looping clip bed (§5e): `gif`

**Use when** the claim has no coordinates and no artefact (psychology, biology, game theory): a looping clip or GIF carries **rhythm**, text and voice carry **facts**.

## Rules

1. **The clip is not evidence.** It carries rhythm and mood, never a claim. A clip that
   "looks like a lab" proves nothing about a real lab; using it as if it did is lying
   with a picture.
2. **The headline never copies the line being read** (§7c). It does what the voice
   cannot: digits, years, source names. Nothing to add: leave it empty.
3. **A dark scrim is mandatory.** You do not control the clip; it can be white exactly
   where the caption stands, with no error. A gradient plus vignette, darker at the top
   (kicker), the headline band and the caption band. After the owner asked for a darker
   overlay, measured band brightness dropped 15–38% and text read clearly while the clip
   stayed visible.
4. **Drift is non-zero.** A 2 s loop under 12 s of narration on a static frame reads as
   a picture on a wall.
5. **A data figure is an overlay, not a replacement**: bars or a ranked column when the
   point is the *relation* between numbers (600 vs 200 as two bars, not two words). A
   scene with a figure has no headline (they compete); the figure brings its own dark
   backing; it builds bar by bar in the order of the argument; unknown entries in a
   ranking stay **unnamed** cells rather than invented names.

## Split layout: two clips in one scene

Only when the two halves **are the mechanism** (a game with two players). One scrim
over the whole frame, not one per half (or the seam gets double darkness and the two
text zones half). The lower half drifts differently from the upper, or it reads as one
image with a line across. A 2 px seam line in the background colour. **The caption
sits on clip B**, so B's lower part must be dark: check on a rendered still.

## Measure on the RENDERED still, not the raw file

Brightness measured on raw clips flagged three clips; on rendered stills two of the
three were false alarms (the raw file has no scrim, no grade, no text outline). Raw
measurements only choose **where to look**. Measure the real caption band, not a
self-chosen "bottom third".

**Loop count is not flicker.** A 1.1 s clip under a 12.7 s scene is 11.9 loops and
looked like it would strobe; frame-difference spikes showed no peak at the loop period.
An artist-made seamless loop plus continuous drift has no seam. The measure is the jump
at the seam, not scene length / clip length.

## Licences: this is debt, not done

GIF sites are mostly user uploads cut from copyrighted film and TV; the platform grants
nobody a re-publishing right. Prefer cutting loops from CC0 stock video yourself (and
playing them as video, which is sharper than 256-colour GIF). One credit line per clip,
written **before** publishing. A clip of unknown rights is debt, and a video carrying it
is not published until it is paid.
