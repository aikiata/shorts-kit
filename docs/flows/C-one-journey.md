# Flow C: One journey

**Shape:** one continuous move from A to B; facts arrive as the camera passes them → land at the destination

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, leg1, leg2, leg3, destination, share` · spoken numbering: **no. A visible destination replaces it** · has a turn: **yes** · fits visuals: b-roll, illustrated-scene, timeline, archive-plate, satellite-map, globe

## When to use

The subject has **a path**: a route, a river, a supply chain, a signal through a
system, an object through a body. Facts belong to places along it.

## Rules

- **C needs a DESTINATION, and the best one is where the viewer is standing.** One
  video ran a trade route across a continent and landed on an artefact dug up in the
  viewers' own country. Viewers cannot count which reason they are on, but they know
  where the trip is heading. If one leg touches the viewer's world, it is the **last**
  scene: do not spend it early.
- **Show the destination early**, even as a dot at the edge. Without it viewers do
  not know how far is left, and that is when they swipe.
- **One countable axis runs the whole trip** (day, km, year), repeated on frame, and
  the turn lands on the axis's last value.
- The whole video is one continuous move, so the background is **outside** the scene
  fade layer (§12b).

## Traps

- **One fact per leg is a sightseeing tour, not a video that sticks.** A first draft
  had 7 stops and ~20 numbers; the owner "remembered nothing". The rescue: one axis
  ("day 0 → 28 → 369"), cut minor stops, end on a sentence without "if".
- A journey whose legs are narrated as numbered reasons is half A, half C.
