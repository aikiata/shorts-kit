# Flow N: Talking to one person

**Shape:** something in the viewer is misunderstood → why it EXISTS (a function, not a fault) → why it overshoots (design, not breakage) → what it costs → change its MEANING, distinct from changing its label → the clinical line → close

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, origin, alarm, cost, reframe, limits, payoff, share` · spoken numbering: **no** · has a turn: **yes** · fits visuals: text-as-image, b-roll, gif

## When to use

An **experience the viewer already has** (anxiety, procrastination, self-blame,
insomnia), where the error is misunderstanding its **nature**, and there is a mechanism
that explains even the unpleasant part. No mechanism: it is encouragement, not this
kit's job. N never promises a cure; the goal is changing understanding.

## Rules

1. **Second person, spoken style, but clean** for a TTS voice: no spoken fillers.
2. **Short sentences are this register, not a fault.** Default target median ~8–14
   words. Measured on a viral video of this form: median 8. This is the opposite of
   teaching-over-visuals; choosing the wrong register loses listeners (§3b).
3. **Dense address.** The viral reference used "you/your" ~37 times per 1,000 words;
   explainer scripts managed 3–9. That difference is "talking to a person" vs "about
   an issue".
4. **No study numbers, no author names on screen.** A name card ("AUTHOR · 2011")
   turned the frame into a citation slide and the owner asked what it was. If a
   number is needed to check an overclaim, say it as a plain fact in the limits scene.
5. **No cure promises.** Anything that sounds like "do this and it's gone" is wrong.
6. **The limits scene is mandatory**: the line between ordinary experience and a
   disorder, and that the latter is treatable.
7. **Distinguish relabelling from reframing.** Most short videos merge them; doing so
   repeats the error the video criticises.
8. **Cut empty filler sentences** that carry no concrete noun ("The cost is real.").
   They hold a place for an idea the next sentence already says: an outline with skin.
9. **Pair each verdict with a sentence the viewer can check against their own body**
   ("your heart still races, just like before").
10. **Keep the honest "also"** where the video admits what it cannot do ("This doesn't
    erase the feeling either."). It is what makes the video believable.
11. **A bridge sentence before the method**, addressed to the person living it: "If
    you're anxious, there's a method. Not pushing it down. Noticing what it actually
    is." Without it, three explanatory scenes suddenly turn into a lecture.
12. **One metaphor, no queue of case studies.** A 60–90 s short carries one mapping
    (anxiety = a smoke alarm).
13. **A hook with footage opens on something already in motion**, with a one-line label.

## Traps

- **Hype in the wrong place.** Hype of delivery (cost first, a real number instead of
  an adjective, an imperative payoff, contrast rhythm) is allowed; hype of claims and
  adjectives is not. An overclaim gets torn apart in comments once, and the channel
  pays for it.
- **Selling knowledge instead of an action.** Every scene answers "what does the viewer
  do with this?" A scene that can only be followed by "so what?" belongs to flow H.
- **Sliding into "anxiety is good".** The cost scene and the limits scene block it.
- **Retelling an experiment** changes genre mid-video. Don't.
- **Clipped too far**: a run of 4–5-word sentences is flat (§3c rule 7).
