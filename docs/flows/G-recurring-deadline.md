# Flow G: A recurring deadline

**Shape:** a duty falls due again and again → the run of times it was missed → what breaks is not money but a relationship

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, calendar, due1, missed1, missed2, breaks, share` · spoken numbering: **no. The calendar replaces it** · has a turn: **yes** · fits visuals: timeline, archive-plate

## When to use

There is a **measurable deadline** (payday on the 28th, a tax term, a tribute season)
and **several recorded misses**. G is F standing still, with a *calendar* running past
instead of an object.

## Rules

- **The timeline is G's mandatory stage, for a mechanical reason:** a break only reads
  once the regular rhythm has been built before it. One missed date alone says
  nothing; it becomes an event once the viewer has seen the dates kept above it. So G
  never opens on the first miss: it opens on **the calendar**.

## Traps

- **Draw the OBLIGATION, not the PAYMENT, unless each payment has a record.** The
  most dangerous trap, because the regular rhythm is what makes G work. Drawing early
  months as "paid in full" with no record saying so is **inventing with a picture**.
  The axis may assert the duty (that date, every month, a sum was due); whether it was
  paid is marked only where a record exists.
- **What breaks at the end is not the money.** If the conclusion is "and they got
  paid eventually", the topic is not ready. G is worth it when the missed deadline
  damages a **relationship**: who still trusts whom, who seeks a new protector.
- **Do not make the debtor a villain** (§1). A debtor admitting they are broke is the
  strongest detail; bending it into oppression throws it away.
