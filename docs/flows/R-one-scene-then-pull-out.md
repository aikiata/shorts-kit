# Flow R: One small moment, then pull out

**Shape:** one small, human moment (a nap, a missed signal) → how things got there → the moment unfolds in one illustrated place, a time-of-day clock in the corner → the consequence → pull out to the big map and fill what was lost

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, before, camp, setup, moment, aftermath, turn, consequence, payoff, share` · spoken numbering: **no. The time-of-day clock replaces it** · has a turn: **yes** · fits visuals: illustrated-scene, archive-plate, satellite-map, globe

## When to use

A **tiny, concrete moment with a huge consequence**: a general's afternoon nap that
lost a battle and, years later, half a country. The moment is the stage; the scale is
the payoff.

**Rejected alternative:** an 18-minute countdown on a top-down map. It shows the
battle, not the moment, and the human detail disappears.

## Rules

- **The hook names the moment and hints at its scale** in one sentence.
- **One illustrated place** (the camp, the tree, the bridge) holds most scenes; a
  **time-of-day clock** in the corner carries the hours.
- **Small objects carry the plot** (a burned bridge, tall grass): each gets its own
  short scene, so the viewer sees the trap close.
- **Pull out only for the consequence**: the scene shrinks, the map appears, and the
  lost territory fills.
- **Close on the source of the moment**: if the story sounds like the winner's rumour,
  the strongest last line is that the loser wrote it down himself (if the record says so).

## Traps

- The map appearing before the consequence: it competes with the moment.
- Mocking the person (§1): the moment is funny; the person is a person.
