# Flow K: Argument

**Shape:** show ONE sentence everyone has heard → "how many holes does it have?" → hole one, two, three, each with a name and an everyday comparison → summary card → one verdict

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, hole1, hole2, hole3, payoff, share` · spoken numbering: **yes ("the first hole…")** · has a turn: **no** · fits visuals: gif, text-as-image, b-roll

## When to use

A **specific sentence** about a social issue that many people repeat, wrong in ways you
can **name**. What the viewer takes away is not a strange fact but **the names of the
faults**: next time they meet a similar sentence, they spot it themselves. Sign: you
can put the sentence on screen verbatim and count two to four faults of different kinds.

⚠️ **It must be an argument people are having NOW**, in your audience. A video with a
correct frame was rejected as "not worth making". Ask first: are 18–35-year-olds
saying this to each other this week?

## Rules

1. **The thing dissected is ONE sentence, on screen.** Not "many people think…". A real
   quote gets its source; a common saying is labelled as one. Never invent a speaker.
2. **The hook is a counting game**, counted aloud.
3. **Each hole: a name, an everyday comparison, at most one number.** The name is what
   viewers keep, so it stays on frame for the scene (a declared exception to §7c).
   Prefer names that are idioms your audience already uses over academic terms.
4. **Grant the other side its point in ONE fair sentence**, inside the relevant hole,
   then move on. A whole rebuttal scene makes viewers lose count.
5. **End on a summary card and a POSITIVE verdict.** The card is the frame's job; the
   voice does not read it (§3c rule 4). The hole's **name** lands after its evidence
   (§3c rule 3). Do not end on a new question. The verdict is not stronger than the data.
6. **Strong words (if enabled) aim at the sentence, never at the people who say it**:
   they are exactly the viewers you want to persuade.

## Traps

- **Writing a school essay.** Position → argument 1 → argument 2 → rebuttal →
  conclusion left one feeling: "there's a lot of research". "Nothing sticks."
- **Many cases, none a story.** Six cases in four countries in 118 s is a list. One
  central case placed in the hole it breaks; the rest go in the description.
- **Straw man**: the sentence must be what people really say, in their words.
- **Overlapping holes**: two names for one fault. Merge. Three real beats four forced.
- Background footage must not show the people the sentence targets (a poor face under
  "they'll spend it on drink" pins the sentence on a real person).
- Avoid politically dangerous topics in your market.
