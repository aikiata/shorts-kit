# Archive plate (§5c): `archive-plate`

**Use when** the subject is a real ARTEFACT (a document, an old map, a photograph) and the viewer believing it is real is part of the argument. **Not** for footage running under the voice (that is `b-roll`).

## Rules

- **The artefact holds still. Hard cut in, hard cut out.** No slow Ken Burns pan.
- **Provenance on frame, mandatory**: name · **date** · holding institution and
  shelfmark · licence. That block is the whole reason the insert exists. It sits
  **above** the caption band.
- **The date is mandatory, not optional** (§5).
- **Background: light when the plate is a single insert, dark when documents are the
  plot.** A light page reads like a book page in a mostly-other video; cutting
  repeatedly between dark scenes and light plates over two minutes is a series of
  flashes on a phone at night, and the two looked like different videos. In the dark
  variant the artefact sits on a **light bordered card**, or a brown scan dissolves into
  the background and stops reading as an object.
- **Use few.** Three inserts in one video is many; two in one scene only as a
  before/after pair, where the pair is the argument.
- **A plate can prove only what the viewer can READ on frame.** A plate's image area is
  about 940×680: a one-page table (ratio ~1) is readable; a two-page spread is not. Its
  structure shows, no number is legible. The spread proves "these two lines are in the
  same document"; it does not prove a division. Unreadable numbers come with on-frame
  text and a larger crop, or stay out.
- **Do not crop so the line that matters is the last line** of the image; the provenance
  rule under the image cuts through it.
- **Images for a wide card need ~1.9–2.0 aspect**; crop first, do not shrink the slot.
- **Provenance text: one line each**, ~30 characters, or the second line pushes onto
  the image.
- **Highlighter marks: build your own** (a span with a yellow multiply background
  sweeping left to right) rather than pulling a package that needs a different
  Remotion version.
- An artefact the voice never points at is illustration, not evidence (flow E).

## Variant: plate floating over a live background

When the claim **has** a place and the story is that a document about that place is
wrong, cutting to a paper background throws away "where is this". Keep the live world
moving and float the plate over it: provenance **on the card** (the only guaranteed
light surface), the card anchored **at the top** so it does not cover the point it is
about, and never zoom the live background past what its resolution supports. The
plate's image source is a required field: you cannot build a text-only card, which is
the line between evidence with provenance and a floating stats card.

## Licences

Order from §5: PD/CC0 → CC BY → ask first for CC BY-SA → never NC. Museums with CC0
open access, checked by API. A rejected artefact is recorded with its reason.
