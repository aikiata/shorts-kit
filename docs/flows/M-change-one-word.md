# Flow M: Change one word

**Shape:** a sentence the viewer says in their own head → let them hear it once → the fault is not the feeling but the LABEL → change exactly ONE word → measure → state the limits → close

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, inner1, label, inner2, measure, limits, payoff, share` · spoken numbering: **no. The sentence on screen replaces it** · has a turn: **yes** · fits visuals: text-as-image

## When to use

The topic is a **technique viewers can apply to themselves**, and what changes is not
their situation but **what they call it**. Two signs at once: you can write the
sentence viewers say in their heads, first person, in their words ("The exam is
tomorrow."); and changing **one word** in it has a **measurement** showing the outcome
differs. No measurement, no flow M: it is advice, and advice is not this kit's job.

M is the only flow where the viewer is both the thing dissected and the one who gains.

## Rules

1. **The dissected sentence is FIRST PERSON and on screen before the voice says
   anything about it.** The voice does not read it; it talks *about* it (declared
   exception to §7c: the frame is the viewer's words, the voice is the narrator's).
2. **Change ONE word, a word viewers would actually say.** "Worried" → "getting ready"
   is a word swap; "anxiety" → "cognitive reappraisal" is a translation nobody uses.
   The word changes **how the reaction is named**, never denies the feeling: the
   research measured the body's arousal as real; what changes is how it is read.
   "The feeling stays, the name changes", not "the feeling isn't real".
3. **Measure after the change, and say what is measured.** The measurement needs a
   **control arm, ideally what everyone already does**: the turn is that the common
   approach ("try to calm down") does worse.
4. **The safety line comes BEFORE the measurement**, not at the end: not a diagnosis,
   not a treatment; if it persists or blocks daily life, see a professional.
5. **Words no stronger than evidence** (§9); never "cures", "guaranteed", "no more".
6. **Close on a sentence the viewer can say to themselves.**
7. The changed word changes **by picture**: struck out, the new one grows in place.
   One word per video; two words are two videos.

## Traps

- **Reading the on-screen sentence aloud.** Loses the whole flow.
- **Turning the label into a pep talk.** "Worry all you want, you'll make it" denies
  the feeling; if viewers hear "cheer up", the scene is wrong.
- **Diagnosing.** No disorder names for the viewer, no symptom checklists.
- **Explaining the mechanism before the viewer feels anything.** Order: they hear their
  own sentence, then learn the fault is the label.
- **A "life lesson" video**: every scene carries a datum. A scene you can drop without
  losing information is advice: cut it.
- No footage of real anxious faces; text-as-image keeps people out of frame.
