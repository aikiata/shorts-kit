# Flow H: Explain it

**Shape:** set the problem → build it up layer by layer → the hardest part is EXPLAINED, not hidden → close on the thing worth remembering

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, problem, layer1, layer2, hardest, payoff, share` · spoken numbering: **no. The order of questions replaces it** · has a turn: **no (by design)** · fits visuals: gif, archive-plate, text-as-image, illustrated-scene

## When to use

Knowledge whose value is **understanding how it works**, not overturning a belief.
H drops the turn on purpose: forcing a reversal onto a mechanism damages the very
thing that makes it interesting.

## Rules

1. **Do not hide the good part.** In other flows the strongest fact goes last; in H
   the hardest-to-understand fact comes **early**, and the time goes to explaining why.
2. **Fewer numbers, more mechanism.** Numbers prove things to people who already
   understand; they do not teach. A number that does not deepen the mechanism stays in
   the ledger. But a number that *teaches* the mechanism stays: cutting the two
   lengths of a coastline left two speeds in m/year that viewers subtracted the wrong
   way round.
3. **The payoff is the most compact statement of the mechanism**, not a negation. A
   declared exception to §1's payoff formula.

## Traps

- **H slides into a flat lecture.** What holds it is **order**: each scene answers the
  question the previous one just raised in the viewer's head. Reread the narration
  alone and ask at each transition: what is the viewer wondering now? Put a column
  "answers which question" in the brief's scene table.
- **"Don't hide the good part" does not mean answering in the hook.** The mechanism
  comes early; the conclusion "what it is" can wait a scene or two (§3c rule 1).
- The hook still has to grab in the first 3 seconds; a draft that opened with two
  concessions took 13 s to reach its hook.
- When the "why" is still `contested` in your ledger, that usually means you have not
  found enough sources yet, not that you should choose another flow.
- Psychology topics: the "this is not a diagnosis" line comes **early**, and applies
  to images too (no specific faces in behaviour scenes).
