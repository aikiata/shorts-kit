# Flow Q: Line and two clocks

**Shape:** the arrival, and the gap between plan and reality → the rules set at the start → the line drawn leg by leg → two year-clocks run: planned vs actual, labelled MOVING / WAITING → close on the gap in one sentence

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, rules, leg1, leg2, leg3, leg4, homecoming, payoff, share` · spoken numbering: **no. The clocks replace it** · has a turn: **yes** · fits visuals: illustrated-scene, timeline, satellite-map, globe

## When to use

A **planned** long effort whose story is the difference between the plan and what
happened: a walk around the world planned for 12 years that took 28, half of them
spent waiting for paperwork. The route is the spine, and the plan is known from the
start, so showing the whole line early is right (unlike flow P).

## Rules

- **The hook states the gap**: arrival date, total time, and the surprising share
  ("for almost half of those years, he didn't move").
- **The self-imposed rules come early**; every later obstacle is measured against them.
- **One line on one map, drawn leg by leg.** Line style carries mode (solid = walked,
  dashed = swum, arc = flown away to wait).
- **Two clocks in a corner**: planned years and actual years, the actual clock labelled
  **MOVING** or **WAITING**. The clocks replace numbering and make the turn visible
  before it is said.
- **No still scene where nothing moves on the map.** Waiting periods still need motion:
  the flight out and back, the clock running.
- **The payoff restates the gap in one sentence**, ideally the person's own words if the
  record has them.

## Traps

- Scenes with nothing on the map while the voice talks about leaving it.
- Too many countries named per scene (§3d): the line carries geography, the voice
  carries obstacles.
