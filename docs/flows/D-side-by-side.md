# Flow D: Side by side

**Shape:** two things, at true scale → the whole video closes the gap between what the viewer assumes and what the ruler says

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, thing1, thing2, ruler, closing, payoff, share` · spoken numbering: **no** · has a turn: **yes** · fits visuals: timeline, archive-plate, b-roll, illustrated-scene

## When to use

A comparison with **one decisive measurement**. Without it, two things side by side
are just illustration.

## Rules

- Both things on screen **at true scale**, or labelled "not to scale" on frame. D is
  the flow most likely to become a lying picture if scale is wrong.
- The gap between belief and measurement **closes scene by scene**; the turn is the
  moment the two numbers meet, not one flip at the end.
- The narration follows the two things, not a list of reasons.

## Variants that worked

- **Two regimes**: not two objects but two systems doing the same thing to the same
  person; the decisive measurement is a year.
- **Two ledgers**: what one party self-reported vs what an inspector recorded; the
  decisive measurement is a division (779 ÷ 162 = 4.8). If the source rounded wrongly
  ("over 6 times"), say the recomputed number and explain in the description.
- **The boast and the ledger**: what an institution *claimed* vs what it *printed*,
  strongest when both come from the same event.

## Traps

- **D does not run on one side.** A draft showed only the ledger side, six tier-1
  tables in a row, and the owner called it rambling. Missing half makes D degrade into
  a list, and the symptom is that viewers feel it is LONG, not that something is
  missing. The fix was to **add** the other side (116 → 138 s), not to cut.
- Both sides must be on screen at the payoff; leaving the voice to carry the
  comparison alone brings the old problem back.
- Contrast sentences need both sides named and the contrast in the ledger (§3b).
