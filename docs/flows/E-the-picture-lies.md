# Flow E: The picture lies

**Shape:** the subject is the DEPICTION (or the wording), not the thing depicted → what is drawn or written contradicts what is measured

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, picture, number, gap, payoff, share` · spoken numbering: **no** · has a turn: **yes** · fits visuals: archive-plate, timeline, b-roll

## When to use

When **the number in the picture fights the number spoken**, and the fight itself is
the topic. A sign you have one: the same mismatch keeps surfacing as an "incident to
hide" while making other videos (a border 517 km as drawn vs 730 km as stated; an
area of 12.26 vs 14.2 million km²). Three stumbles on the same thing are a topic.

## Variant: the wording lies

The same argument applies to a **description in words**: when a sentence in a record
decided reality and was ambiguous. Example shape: a treaty named "the river" as a
border, there were two rivers, and the strip between them became its own state for
centuries. Two conditions:

- **You can show the exact wording** that caused it. Without the original text, it is
  an anecdote: same reason B needs a walkable chain.
- **The consequence is measurable** in years or metres. "Caused controversy" is not.

## Rules

- **Both numbers need ledger records**, because the video says they differ.
- **Evidence first, explanation after.** Plot the data points, then lay the
  contradicting depictions over them. Drawing your corridor first and sprinkling dots
  on it turns evidence into illustration of your own drawing.
- Hand-drawn lines are **re-checked against the data**; a corridor drawn off the
  cluster of points is the picture fighting the voice, and no command catches it.
- The depiction must match the era of the story; a modern-style chart for a
  19th-century claim contradicts itself.

## Traps

- E needs two layers in one frame, so on-frame text and chart labels fight for space;
  plan which layer yields.
- An artefact on screen that the voice never points at is illustration, not evidence:
  viewers asked "I still don't see the two islands" until a scene said it aloud.
