# Flow F: Crossing the border

**Shape:** something arrives → expensive and foreign → gets modified → becomes local identity

**Beats** (scene ids `bun run new` scaffolds): `hook, arrival, expensive, modified, identity, payoff, share` · spoken numbering: **no. Spoken years replace it** · has a turn: **yes** · fits visuals: timeline, archive-plate

## When to use

Food, a script, an instrument, a crop: something with an **arrival date**.

## Rules

- **F tells in TIME, not in space.** C moves from A to B; F stands still and lets the
  years run past. Its stage is the timeline. Decide that at the brief.
- **The countable marker is the years said aloud** ("1859… 1958… today"), and the
  axis on frame matches exactly those years: countable on one hand.

## Traps

- **"Turns out it's foreign" is not the turn; it is the opening.** Viewers already
  know the dish has a foreign origin. The turn is the moment it **stopped being
  foreign**: where the recipe changed, the price fell, locals took it and made it
  differently. Find that link first.
- **F's engine is an economic number, not a feeling.** "Luxury became everyday" is
  just words until there is a price, an import volume, a count of shops. No number,
  not ready.
- **F slides into national pride more easily than any flow**, and §1 forbids it.
  "They turned it into a masterpiece" is a travel article. Tell what happened.
