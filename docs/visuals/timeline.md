# Timeline (§5b): `timeline`

**Use when** the stage is YEARS, not a place: a chain of dates, the gap between two, a list that grows over time. The axis is drawn to true scale.

## Rules

- **The timeline is the STAGE, not an insert.** It enters at the hook, stays to the
  payoff, and the camera travels on it. An axis that appears for three seconds is a
  stats card. If it can only carry one scene, the topic is not a timeline topic.
- **Vertical.** A 9:16 frame has 1080 px across and 1920 down; two thousand years
  across the narrow side stacks four labels on top of each other. Time flows top to
  bottom, the camera drifts down; years on a left rail, artefacts and numbers open to
  the right, inside the safe box (§7a) and above the caption band.
- **True scale, or "not to scale" on frame.** No third option. And for time-compression
  topics, the imbalance **is** the content (an ancient queen closer to the moon landing
  than to the pyramids only means something on a true axis). Crowded marks are solved
  by **zooming the camera onto the axis**, never by stretching the crowded part.
- **Every mark is a `verified` record with a year.** Without a firm year, draw a
  **span** across the uncertain range, never a fake tick. What you would not say aloud,
  you do not draw as a tick.
- **Countable on one hand.** Seven marks are no longer countable, and lose their
  retention role.
- **Artefacts anchor to their mark**, below its label, so a 13th-century textile behind
  a 6th-century story visibly sits seven centuries away.
- **Show the whole span before running on it**; the viewer needs both ends to read a
  dot's position.
- **The axis moves, labels do not dance.** Spoken year + axis year + headline year is
  one thing said three times: keep the headline for the strange half, or empty.
- Reading time ≥ 6 s for an axis with two marks (§3).

## Lessons from renders

- **A wide shot cannot read every mark: do not try.** Keep the ticks, drop labels
  closer than ~52 px to the previous one (except the active mark).
- **Spans get their own lane**; drawn on the axis line they swallow it and every tick
  inside.
- **Every threshold must FADE, not switch**, once the view moves: marks leaving the
  view, labels hidden for spacing, gap notes. Boolean thresholds are right on a still
  and jerk on a moving camera. Leave margin outside the outermost marks, or edge fades
  dim them even on a static frame. A span does not fade by distance to the edge.
- **A dashed "gap" corridor says "WE DON'T KNOW", not "NOTHING HAPPENED".** Using it for
  thirty well-documented years (a book banned from reprint) is lying with the vocabulary
  of ignorance. Known stretch → a **span**; stretch without data → a **corridor**. Never
  both for the same years. The corridor done right was the strongest still of all: "War,
  partition, rationing. Forty-seven years we could not check anything."
